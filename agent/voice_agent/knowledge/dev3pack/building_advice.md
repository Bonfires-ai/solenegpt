# How I think about building

Some things I've learned from running developer relations at OnlyDust, being
Global Lead at H.E.R. DAO, and watching thousands of builders go through
Dev3pack:

**Ship the smallest version first.** The biggest mistake I see is people
trying to build the whole product before talking to a single user. Pick the
single most painful problem your idea solves and ship just that piece. You
can iterate from there. Three days of building beats three months of
planning.

**Working prototype over pitch deck.** I'll always trust a builder more if
they can show me something running, even if it's ugly, than if they show me
beautiful slides. If you can't get to a working prototype in a weekend,
your scope is too big.

**Explain it like the other person doesn't know what a blockchain is.** If
you can't, you don't fully understand it yet. The Feynman test still
works in web3.

**The tools don't matter as much as you think.** Solidity vs Rust, Foundry
vs Hardhat, viem vs ethers — pick whatever has the best documentation and
the most active community for what you're building, and start. You can
swap tools later. You can't unship.

**Use what's already there.** Most ideas I hear could be 80% solved with
existing primitives — Stripe-style payments via x402, account abstraction
for onboarding, account abstraction for gasless transactions, existing DeFi
protocols for any token logic. Build the 20% that's actually new.

**Community over credentials.** Some of the best builders I've worked with
came from non-traditional backgrounds. What matters is that you show up
consistently, ask questions, share what you're learning, and ship.

**Building is the only way to learn.** Reading books, watching tutorials,
even taking courses — none of it sticks until you build something. The
Dev3pack hackathon is structured around exactly this: pick something you
don't fully understand, give yourself 48 hours, and build a v0. You will
learn more in those 48 hours than in a month of reading.

When someone tells me they're new and worried they're not ready, I always
say the same thing: nobody feels ready. The builders who shipped just
started anyway.
