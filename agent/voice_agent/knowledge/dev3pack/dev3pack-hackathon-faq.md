## 🌍 General

### What is the Dev3Pack Global Hackathon?

The Dev3Pack Global Hackathon is a hybrid, three-day Web3 & AI student hackathon running across 6 continents. Participants build, demo, and compete for prizes, with all projects deployed on Solana. It's part of a broader Dev3Pack journey that includes a pre-hackathon bootcamp, a global demo day, student club support, and a startup accelerator.

### Where does it take place?

It's a hybrid event. You can hack online from anywhere or join one of 50+ official local hubs in cities and universities worldwide. The website has a "Find a Hub Near You" section to locate the closest one.

### Do I need to hack in person (IRL)?

No. In-person attendance at a hub is **completely optional**. You can do the entire hackathon — kickoff, building, feedback, submission, closing — fully online. Local hubs are there if you want the energy, the hallway conversations, and a real human support system, but they're not required to participate or win.

### Can I start building my project before the hackathon?

**Yes, with one condition: the project must be related to Solana or AI.** You're encouraged to come in with prior code, designs, and research as long as the work is in the Solana ecosystem. The Vibe Coding Bootcamp (May 3–7) is specifically there to help you ramp up in the days leading into the kickoff. What matters at submission is that the project is yours and meets the track's qualification requirements.

### Does my project have to be open source?

Yes, it is mandatory.

### Where can I find the recordings of the Vibe Coding Bootcamp workshops?

All bootcamp workshops are recorded and available on YouTube here: [**Vibe Coding Bootcamp playlist**](https://youtube.com/playlist?list=PLvimYWRMGIoWDEJdo-tmxU-iX_WvfRPEC&si=pbbxyNEzEkb8vzRJ). If you missed a session live, or want to rewatch one before submission, the playlist is the place to go.

### I applied but didn't get accepted yet — how do I check?

You may have missed the confirmation email (check spam, promotions, and "all mail"). The fastest way to check your status is to try creating your account here: [**hack.dev3pack.xyz/auth/sign-up**](https://hack.dev3pack.xyz/auth/sign-up).

- If your email is **whitelisted**, the sign-up will go through — you've been accepted, you're in.
- If it's **not whitelisted**, the sign-up will be blocked — you haven't been accepted (yet).

Use the **same email you applied with**. If sign-up is blocked but you're confident you applied, double-check the email address and reach out via the [Telegram group](https://t.me/+k1f-taBk5JU5ODk0).

### What's the best way to get started with the hackathon?

The fastest path from zero to a working Solana app, in three steps:

1. **Explore ideas** — use the resources in the "💡 I don't know what to build — help!" section below (ideas.sendai.fun, Colosseum Copilot, Superteam Build, past winners). Pick one that excites you.
2. **Prompt it** — write a clear prompt describing what you want to build: the user, the problem, the core flow, and any Solana / partner integrations you want (LI.FI, Solana Mobile, ElevenLabs, etc.).
3. **Paste it into [trynoah.ai](https://trynoah.ai/)** — Noah AI will generate a ready-to-go full-stack Solana app you can clone, run, and start customizing in minutes.

From there, you have a working baseline and can spend your hackathon hours on what makes your project unique instead of plumbing.

## 👥 Teams

### Can I hack solo?

Yes. Solo participation is fully allowed. You're eligible for all the same prizes and perks.

### How big can my team be?

Teams can have a **maximum of 4 members**. Solo (1 person) is the minimum.

### How do I form a team?

There are three ways:

1. **Create a Team** — pick a name, add a short description of what you're building, tag the roles you're looking for (Frontend, Backend, Smart Contract, AI Engineer, UI/UX Designer, etc.), and choose whether to make it open to new members.
2. **Join by Code** — ask a teammate for their 6-character join code and enter it.
3. **Browse the Participant's Directory** — find people by name, city, university, country, background, or language, and reach out to those marked "Looking for team."

### Can I join multiple teams?

No, you can only be part of one team at a time.

### Can I switch teams?

Yes, but talk to your current team first. You'll need to leave your current team before joining or creating a new one.

### I'm looking for a team — what should I do?

A few quick steps to find one fast:

1. In your profile, toggle on **"Looking for team"** so you show up with the green badge in the Participant's Directory.
2. Open the **Directory** tab and filter by country, background, or language to find compatible hackers.
3. Browse the **Open Teams** list (teams that ticked "Open to new members") and request to join one whose project sounds interesting.
4. Show up to the **Idea Brainstorming & Team Formation** session on Fri 8 May, 15:00–16:00 UTC — it's specifically built for matching solo hackers into teams.
5. If you're at a local hub, ask the hub organizer — they usually know who's still looking.

### What if I can't find teammates?

Worst case: hack solo. You're eligible for every prize a team is, and many great hackathon projects have been shipped by one person.

## 🛠️ Project Submission

### What's the submission deadline?

The hard deadline is **08:00 UTC on Sunday, May 10, 2026**. The submission window opens at 07:00 UTC the same day.

### Can I edit my project after I create it?

**Yes — until the deadline.** You can keep editing every part of your submission (name, description, images, links, video, team, track selections) right up to **08:00 UTC on Sun 10 May**. After the deadline, submissions are locked and judging begins.

### Can I save a draft before the submission window opens?

Yes. Even when submissions are closed, you can save your project as a draft and return to finish it later.

### What do I need to submit?

The submission form has 5 steps:

1. **Project** — name, one-liner, optional category, optional smart contract/program address, optional local hub, and description.
2. **Images** — visuals of your project.
3. **Links & video** — demo video, repo, live link, etc.
4. **Team** — confirm your team members.
5. **Review & submit** — final check before submitting.

### What's required for the demo video?

- **Length: under 3 minutes — strict.** Videos longer than 3 minutes are **disqualified from prize eligibility**. Judges will not watch past the 3-min mark, and the video being over the limit is one of the hard disqualifiers in the rubric. If your demo runs long, cut it.
- **Content:** show the product in action — a real demo, not a pitch deck. Walk through the user flow and clearly explain how each track-specific integration works (LI.FI, Solana Mobile, ElevenLabs, etc.) if you're applying.
- **Hosting:** upload to YouTube, Loom, or another publicly accessible link (no login walls).
- **Language:** any language is welcome — submit in whatever lets you best showcase your work. Adding English subtitles is a nice-to-have for global judges but not required. READMEs in any language are also accepted.

A great demo answers: *what does it do, who is it for, and how does it use Solana?* in under 3 minutes.

### How are projects judged?

Judging happens in two stages, using two rubrics: **Product** and **Technical**. Each weights a few factors out of 100%.

**Stage 1 — all submissions are scored** to filter the field down to the top 100 (Solana main track). Sponsor tracks (LI.FI, Virtuals, Solana Mobile, ElevenLabs) use Stage 1 only.

**Stage 2 — the top 100 get a deeper review**, including code-level inspection of the Solana/Rust program, founder-market fit, and end-to-end live demo verification.

**Product Rubric — what judges look for:**
- **Eligibility compliance** — meeting the basics (public repo, demo URL, video under 3 min). Missing any of these is a hard disqualifier.
- **Product clarity** — is the problem clear, the solution direct, and the target user obvious from the demo alone?
- **Real-world impact and utility** — real pain point, plausible adoption path, beyond boilerplate.
- **Demo and pitch quality** — focused pitch, differentiators highlighted, demo actually works.
- **Inclusivity and diversity** — used as a positive modifier (e.g., teams with women builders, geographic distribution across the top 100).

**Technical Rubric — what judges look for:**
- **Deployment and verification** — contract address present, base58 format, devnet or mainnet. No deployed contract = ranked but not top-100 eligible.
- **Repository and code quality** — README present, coherent repo structure; for top 100, readable code, tests / deploy scripts, intentional architecture.
- **Solana and SDK depth** — README names the framework (Anchor / Pinocchio / Quasar / vanilla Rust), valid base58 address, Cargo.toml references the right crates. Depth of SDK usage across the codebase is a bonus.
- **Track-specific requirements** — each partner track has its own checks (LI.FI integration is real and not cosmetic, ElevenLabs has ≥1 integration path documented, Solana Mobile produces a functional APK with Mobile Wallet Adapter and is dApp-Store-submitted, Robotics shows an actual robot in the demo, etc.).

The full detailed rubrics are maintained by the judging team and may be updated before the hackathon — the points above cover what you need to optimize for.

### Who judges?

Judges are mentors and industry professionals from Solana, the partner companies (LI.FI, Virtuals, Solana Mobile, ElevenLabs), and the wider Web3 / AI ecosystem. Mixed-role teams and multilingual submissions are explicitly welcome.

### Are all projects required to use Solana?

Yes, if you want to be eligible for prizes and accelerator. You can also opt into additional partner tracks (LI.FI, Virtuals, Solana Mobile, ElevenLabs) when submitting.

### Can I build a project on another blockchain?

The hackathon is on Solana, so a project built on Ethereum or any other non-Solana chain **will be disqualified for prizes.** If your idea uses Ethereum tooling, port the core to Solana, or use a partner like LI.FI to bridge — but the deployed program/contract being judged must live on Solana (mainnet or devnet).

### Mainnet or devnet?

Either works — both mainnet and devnet smart contract / program addresses are accepted.

### What if I'm hacking in person?

Pick your city hub from the "Submitting from a local hub?" dropdown — it's auto-detected based on your location, but you can change it.

## 💡 I don't know what to build — help!

Don't try to invent something from scratch under pressure. Use these idea sources, in order:

1. [**ideas.sendai.fun**](https://ideas.sendai.fun/) — curated AI agent and Solana project ideas from SendAI.
2. [**Colosseum Copilot**](https://colosseum.com/copilot) — pressure-test your idea against 5,400+ past hackathon submissions and live ecosystem data so you don't accidentally rebuild something that already exists.
3. [**Superteam Build — Ideas**](https://superteam.fun/build/ideas) — inspiration for DeFi, NFTs, payments, consumer dApps, and more.
4. [**Past Global Hackathon Winners**](https://superteam.fun/build/past-hackathon-winners) — see what's actually won at global Solana hackathons. Great for understanding the bar and finding directions worth exploring.

A practical approach:

- Spend ~30 min on ideas.sendai.fun and Superteam Build to write down 5 candidate ideas.
- Run each idea through Colosseum Copilot to see what's already been built.
- Pick the one that overlaps with your team's skills and gets you most excited — excitement carries you through hour 40.

## 📜 Code of Conduct & Rules

### Are AI-generated code and tools allowed?
**Yes — encouraged.** This is a vibe coding hackathon. Cursor, Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, Solana skills, MCP servers — use whatever helps you ship faster. The Vibe Coding Bootcamp is built around exactly this. What matters is that the project is yours, you understand it, and you can demo and explain it.

### What about plagiarism and reusing code?
- **Open-source libraries and templates are fine** — that's how everyone builds.
- **Forking someone else's hackathon project and submitting it as yours is not** — it'll be disqualified.
- **Pre-existing code from your own projects is allowed** if it's Solana or AI related (see "Can I start building before the hackathon?"). Be transparent in the README about what was built before vs. during the hackathon.

### Behavior expectations
Be respectful. Be inclusive. Help other hackers when you can. Harassment, discrimination, or toxic behavior of any kind — at hubs, in the chat, or in mentor sessions — gets you removed from the hackathon and any future Dev3Pack programs.

### Eligibility
The hackathon is designed for students and young builders transitioning into Web3. By submitting a project you confirm that everyone on your team is aware of and agrees to the code of conduct and the track qualification requirements.
